## ReadMeABook and SeerrNG

ReadMeABook's feature is **BookDate**. Its [feature document](https://github.com/kikootwo/ReadMeABook/blob/71fc762ff6e176720914c3c4ba33ab347b1f7af3/documentation/features/bookdate.md), [recommendation card](https://github.com/kikootwo/ReadMeABook/blob/71fc762ff6e176720914c3c4ba33ab347b1f7af3/src/components/bookdate/RecommendationCard.tsx) and [page](https://github.com/kikootwo/ReadMeABook/blob/71fc762ff6e176720914c3c4ba33ab347b1f7af3/src/app/bookdate/page.tsx) show personalized cached audiobook suggestions, swipe/button decisions, request confirmation and undo. Its purpose is acquisition; Plezy's first pool should instead be accessible media the user already has. Adopt the low-effort choice loop, then independently design around Plezy's native controls, neutral skipping, durable recovery and consumption routes. Do not copy code or visual branding for this plan. Confidence: high in the observed mechanics; moderate in their relevance to this product.

SeerrNG's [Swipe guide](https://github.com/snapetech-org/seerrng/blob/a0fb796b118135cc0eb4245994d294fc754d6a9f/docs/using-seerr/swipe.md) and [screen implementation](https://github.com/snapetech-org/seerrng/blob/a0fb796b118135cc0eb4245994d294fc754d6a9f/src/components/Swipe/index.tsx) show a request-oriented category rail, search, filters, preferences, history and service-specific request choices. The user explicitly rejected its clunky feel for PlezyNG. No specific prior usability failure report was recovered; do not invent one. Our design inference is to keep one compact context sheet, separate local discovery from acquisition, and use the app's existing consumption surfaces. Confidence: high on the code/documented structure; moderate on which simplification will most improve perceived ease.

The two products also differ in scope. SeerrNG can request categories Plezy does not consume. Copying that entire category list would misrepresent Plezy's capabilities. Conversely, filtering out every already-owned title would remove the most useful candidates for a playback/reading client.

## Official interaction guidance

| Primary source | Relevant guidance | Plan consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Flutter accessibility](https://docs.flutter.dev/ui/accessibility) | Label controls, test real screen readers and large scale, provide adequate targets and recoverable actions | Visible buttons/semantics, large-text reflow, persistent Undo, device evidence |
| [W3C dragging movements](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html) | A dragging action needs a single-pointer alternative; keyboard access alone is not that alternative | Every swipe action has a directly clickable/tappable labelled control |
| [Android TV buttons](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/tv/guides/components/buttons) | Clear action labels, restrained number of controls, predictable placement and a clear primary action | Stable remote focus/action row with one prominent consumption action; no swipe gesture required |

These are interaction inputs, not a claim of blanket accessibility certification. Apple's focus guidance was located but its body was not retrievable through the research service; it is not used as evidence for a specific requirement here. TV-specific behavior is grounded in the existing Plezy focus infrastructure and the retrieved Android guidance, with actual tvOS testing required.

## Deliberate proposals, not sourced facts

The name Find your next; mixed activity intent; neutral Not now; local Saved picks; moment-based consumption units; safe samples; verified alternate formats; co-present shortlist voting; initial rank weights; eight-card/three-save checkpoint; gesture thresholds; retention sizes and performance budgets are proposed design decisions. They are not copied market statistics or proven uplift claims.

Expected benefits: less setup, fewer ambiguous actions, fewer irrelevant or unplayable suggestions and easier recovery. Confidence: moderate until measured. Competitive novelty and commercial impact: unknown. Source/device correctness can be validated much more strongly than subjective delight; both need their own acceptance evidence.
